# State checkpoint nudges as declarative rules

- **Date:** 2026-06-29
- **Governs:** `plugins/*/hooks/`, `plugins/*/skills/`, `plugins/skill-enforcer/hooks/sef-hook.js`

Hooks and skills nudge the model at lifecycle checkpoints. A nudge states one declarative rule and asks the model to
emit nothing, at the cost of no visible evidence that the checkpoint fired.

## Decision

- **One declarative rule.** A nudge states one behavioral rule that constrains the action. It asks for no artifact: no
  tag-shaped template, no fill-in fields, no scripted self-questions.
- **Silent apply.** Each reminder ends with a contract to act on the rule and never echo it or narrate the check.
- **Scope.** The rule covers every skill and hook nudge in the marketplace, not only skill-enforcer's.

## Why

- Through skill-enforcer v1.5.0 each reminder handed the model a `<thinking><sef-eval>` skeleton, and on reasoning
  models (Opus 4.8 and later) the tags leaked into the visible reply: the native reasoning channel is not addressable by
  typing `<thinking>` (2026-06-29).
- At low and medium effort the model may skip the thinking block on a step, so an artifact meant for it spilled into the
  reply or was dropped.
- Reasoning models follow a constraint stated declaratively more reliably than one performed as a procedural self-Q&A,
  and prescriptive chain-of-thought scaffolding degrades their instruction-following.

## Consequences

- No visible evaluation confirms that a checkpoint fired; the evidence is whether skills get invoked. Accepted.
- A declarative nudge can under-trigger against a forced ceremony, so tuning measures the invocation rate, not only the
  absence of leaks.

## Rejected

- **A structured evaluation emitted in the reasoning stream** — leaked into visible output on reasoning models, and had
  nowhere to land when no thinking block ran.

## Revisit if

The host gives the model an addressable private channel for structured self-evaluation. Confidence not recorded.
